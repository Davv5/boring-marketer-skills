# Email copy principles and per-email checks

Read by the write-emails step before drafting any email; apply the per-email checks to every email before saving.

## The P.S. is prime real estate

40% of people read the P.S. first. Use it for:
- The core CTA
- A second hook
- Personal note
- Deadline reminder

## One CTA per email

Multiple CTAs = no CTAs. Every email should have ONE clear action.

Exception: Delivery email can have "download" + "reply with question"

## Short paragraphs

1-3 sentences max. Email is scanned, not read.

## Preview text matters

First 40-90 characters appear in inbox preview. Make them count.

**Bad:** "Having trouble viewing this email?"
**Good:** "[Continuation of subject line curiosity]"

## Open loops

Create curiosity within emails:
- "I'll explain why tomorrow."
- "But that's not even the interesting part."
- "The third one surprised me."

## Specificity creates credibility

- Not "made money" -- "$47,329 in one day"
- Not "many customers" -- "2,847 customers"
- Not "recently" -- "Last Tuesday"

## Per-email checks

- 3 subject line variants (safe bet, bold play, personal), each from a different formula category
- Preview text that complements the subject
- A single CTA
- One clear purpose
- P.S. used strategically (or omitted on purpose)
- An open loop to the next email (except the final email)
- A specific send day and time with rationale
- Voice matching the brand profile (when loaded)
- Saved as an individual file with the naming convention
