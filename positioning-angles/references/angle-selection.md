# Positioning angle selection

## The angle-finding process

Load this reference during angle generation and for returning-run decisions. It preserves the skill-specific angle generators, checks, and update workflow.

## The core job

Surface multiple powerful options rather than claim there is one correct answer. The best angle depends on the audience and market moment. Present 3–5 distinct options; each has a statement, why it works for this audience, headline direction, and best-fit conditions. Recommend exactly one.

## Angle generators

Use multiple lenses; keep only the strongest distinct options. Example formulations are starting points, not claims to copy.

### The Contrarian Angle
Challenge a market belief that may be false (for example, "Stop [common practice]; here's what works"). Use when the audience is frustrated with convention and values independent thinking.

### The Unique Mechanism Angle
Lead with how the product works, naming a real process or insight ("The [named method] that [specific result]"). Use when similar promises abound, especially in sophisticated markets. Load `unique-mechanism.md` for discovery and substantiation.

### The Transformation Angle
Make the before/after gap concrete ("From [current state] to [desired outcome]"). Use when the change is dramatic, specific, and the market recognizes the problem.

### The Enemy Angle
Rally against a shared problem, mindset, or obstacle rather than a competitor. Use when the audience shares a clear frustration or villain.

### The Speed/Ease Angle
Reduce time or effort ("[Outcome] without [expected sacrifice]"). Use only when speed or ease is genuinely differentiated and alternatives impose meaningful effort.

### The Specificity Angle
Name a sharply defined audience or result. Use to signal a generic market that the offer is built for a particular person or need.

### The Social Proof Angle
Lead with credible, specific evidence rather than unsupported promises. Use when proof is strong and trust is the primary barrier.

### The Risk Reversal Angle
Lead with a guarantee. Use when risk is the main objection and the seller can confidently honor the guarantee.

The additional classic copywriting frameworks in `angle-frameworks.md` are useful when these lenses need further development.

## The test

Check each candidate:

1. **Specificity:** reject vague benefits; make the result concrete without fabricating metrics.
2. **Differentiation:** compare with the competitive landscape; sharpen any claim a competitor already makes.
3. **Believability:** require a supporting mechanism, proof, or appropriately qualified hypothesis.
4. **Audience relevance:** verify fit with loaded audience segments and pain points; beginner language may fail for experts.
5. **Usability:** ensure a headline and downstream copy can follow from it.

## Iteration and update mode

When `./brand/positioning.md` exists, show the current primary angle, statement, and last-updated date, then offer refine or first run from scratch. Preserve the current file until the user confirms a replacement.

### Display current state

Show the current primary angle, statement, last-updated date, and the choice to refine or start fresh.

### Refine mode

1. Load saved angles and run fresh competitive web research.
2. Compare the new landscape with the saved one.
3. Identify new competitors, newly saturated claims, and new white space.
4. State whether the current angle still has room, needs sharpening, or a new opportunity supports a pivot. Do not recommend change without a reason.
5. Present 1–3 refinements beside the original; let the user keep, tweak, or replace it.

### Start fresh mode

Run the full skill process; preserve existing positioning until explicit replacement confirmation.

## What this skill is NOT

Activate when the user asks how to position an offer, find its angle or hook, diagnose weak sales, differentiate it, or establish positioning before writing a landing page or copy. A downstream skill may invoke this work when it needs a strategic angle.

This skill provides strategic direction, not finished copy, landing-page structure, audience research from scratch, or a single mandatory answer. It performs a messaging-focused competitor scan, not a deep competitor teardown. Assume the user knows the audience unless context is available or questions are needed.
