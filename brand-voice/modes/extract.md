# Extract Mode

Use when the user has existing content they are proud of: website copy, emails, social posts, newsletters, video transcripts, or other material that sounds like them. For a returning run, use this Mode to incorporate new samples into the existing profile.

## What to Analyze

Request 3–5 pieces they consider “most them,” such as:
- Website copy, especially About page or homepage
- Emails they have sent
- Social posts that performed well
- Newsletter editions
- Video or podcast transcripts
- Anything where they felt “this sounds like me”

## What to Look For

1. **Tone patterns:** Formal ↔ Casual (contractions, slang, fragments); Serious ↔ Playful (humor, lightness, gravity); Reserved ↔ Bold (hedging, strong claims, confidence); Distant ↔ Intimate (we/they vs I/you, personal stories).
2. **Vocabulary patterns:** Industry jargon level (heavy, light, translated); repeated signature words or phrases; words they avoid; profanity or edgy language; formal words versus everyday words.
3. **Rhythm patterns:** Average sentence length, paragraph length, mix of short punchy and longer flowing sentences, fragments, and list usage.
4. **Structural patterns:** How they open (story, question, statement), transition, and close (CTA, summary, open loop); headers and formatting preferences.
5. **Personality signals:** Self-deprecating or confident; teacher or peer; polished or raw; optimistic or realistic; references and examples used.
6. **POV patterns:** First person (I) or plural (we); reader address (you, folks, friends); direct address or general statements.

Separate repeated observations from one-off choices. Capture examples that demonstrate each pattern and identify contradictory evidence instead of smoothing it away.

## Extract Output

Use the observations to produce the profile with the template referenced by the main skill, then run its Voice Test Loop.

**Done:** 3–5 representative pieces have been analyzed across all six dimensions, with grounded profile observations and any evidence gaps called out.
