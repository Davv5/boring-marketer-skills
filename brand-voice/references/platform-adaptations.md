# Platform adaptation guide

Read while completing the Platform Adaptations table. These are starting considerations, not defaults to copy: derive each brand's shift from its evidence and confirm unsupported choices with the user.

| Platform | Tone shift | Structure | Length and adaptations |
|---|---|---|---|
| Email | Warmer and more personal: a 1:1 conversation with someone who gave you their address | Short paragraphs (1–3 sentences), one clear CTA, personal greeting when appropriate | 150–300 words for regular sends; up to 500 for story-driven sequences. More contractions and “I”/“you”; “And” or “But” sentence openings can fit. Match sign-off to personality. |
| LinkedIn | More professional and expertise-forward, not stiff; lead with insight rather than personality | One idea per post, line breaks between thoughts, hook before “see more” | 100–200 words regular, up to 300 deep posts. Fewer contractions than email; framework language where natural; close with a question or engagement CTA; avoid hashtag spam. |
| Twitter/X | Punchiest, shorter, more opinionated when authentic; maximum signal | One idea per post; threads for longer stories, with each post standing alone; no fluffy intros | Under 280 characters per post; 5–15 posts per thread. Fragments and single-sentence posts can fit; use strong openings, numbers, and specifics. |
| Blog/SEO | Same personality with more depth; teach, share, or argue without performing | Headings, subheads, lists, 3–5-sentence paragraphs; examples and proof throughout | 1,500–2,500 words standard; up to 4,000 for pillar content. Allow nuance; explain jargon; maintain voice rhythm at length. |
| Landing page | Most urgent and benefit-focused; sales voice with brand personality | Short sentences, benefit-led headlines, social proof blocks, repeated clear CTAs; above-the-fold hook must land | Varies by section: hero 20–40 words, feature sections 50–100 each, full page 800–2,000. Make pain points explicit and proof prominent; stronger CTAs and power words only where on-brand. |
